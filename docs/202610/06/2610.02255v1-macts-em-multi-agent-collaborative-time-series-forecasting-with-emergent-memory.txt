MACTS-EM: Multi-Agent Collaborative Time
Series Forecasting with Emergent Memory
Ahmad Shahi1 and Mamehgol Yousefi1
Unitec Institute of Technology, Auckland, New Zealand
{sahmad,myousefidashliboroun}@unitec.ac.nz
Abstract. Time series forecasting remains a critical challenge across nu-
merous domains. Despite significant advancements, existing approaches
struggle with complex phenomena such as regime shifts, cross-domain
knowledge transfer, and multimodal data integration. This paper intro-
duces Multi-Agent Collaborative Time Series Forecasting with Emer-
gent Memory (MACTS-EM), a novel framework where specialised agents
collaborate to achieve superior forecasting performance. The MACTS-
EM architecture integrates: (1) domain-specialised forecasting agents
for pattern recognition, anomaly detection, causal inference, and un-
certainty quantification; (2) a meta-cognitive layer for dynamic agent
allocation; (3) an emergent memory mechanism enabling cross-domain
pattern transfer; (4) multimodal contextual integration; and (5) adver-
sarial robustness components. Evaluation across financial markets, cli-
mate patterns, energy consumption, and pandemic propagation demon-
strates that MACTS-EM outperforms existing approaches in most sce-
narios, with 8–12% improvement in forecasting accuracy, 22–27% better
zero-shot transfer capability, 16–21% enhanced resilience during regime
shifts, and 15–18% faster recovery after distribution shifts. Our findings
suggest that collaborative, agentic approaches to time series forecasting
represent a promising direction beyond traditional architectures, particu-
larly for complex real-world scenarios requiring multi-resolution temporal
understanding and contextual adaptation.

Introduction
Time series forecasting is a critical challenge across numerous real-world dy-
namic systems [5] in finance [17], healthcare [1], climate science [13], and energy
management [12]. Despite extensive research, existing approaches struggle with
highly dynamic and interconnected systems [26], particularly with complex phe-
nomena such as regime shifts, cross-domain knowledge transfer, and multimodal
data integration [21].
Most forecasting models operate as monolithic frameworks (e.g., LSTMs,
Transformers) [18] that process entire temporal sequences simultaneously rather

than using specialised components for different temporal dynamics. Recent ad-
vances include foundation models like TimeGPT [8], Chronos [2], and Time-

tively. While architectures such as TimeMixer [21], TEMPO [4], and TiDE [6]
capture multi-scale patterns, they generally operate as single models without
specialised expertise.
In contrast, multi-agent systems have demonstrated success in solving com-
plex problems through collaborative interaction [16, 23]. Unlike traditional en-
sembles that merely aggregate outputs, these systems employ complex com-
munication and knowledge sharing mechanisms [25]. However, their application
to specialised domains like time series forecasting remains limited [3]. Simi-
larly, while transfer learning approaches have been applied within domains [15]
through domain-adversarial networks [7] and meta-learning [19], cross-domain
knowledge transfer remains challenging for time series with fundamentally dif-
ferent temporal dynamics [22].
Large language models (LLMs) have recently shown remarkable capabili-
ties in developing collaborative multi-agent frameworks [10, 20]. These models
can perform complex tasks through task decomposition, memory systems, and
multi-step reasoning [24], suggesting their potential to reconceptualise time series
forecasting through an agent-based lens. Their pattern recognition capabilities
could enable highly precise forecasts, while multimodal LLMs could leverage di-
verse knowledge types for synergistic forecasting [9]. Unlike specialised modelling
approaches requiring significant parameter tuning, LLMs trained on massive
datasets can be adapted to forecasting tasks without learning from scratch.
In this work, we propose Multi-Agent Collaborative Time Series Forecast-
ing with Emergent Memory (MACTS-EM), a novel paradigm that reimagines
forecasting as a collaborative process among specialised agents coordinated by a
meta-cognitive layer and an emergent memory mechanism. MACTS-EM is fun-
damentally distinct from traditional ensemble methods in three key aspects: (1)
it implements a dynamic, interactive collaboration process where agents com-
municate intermediate representations; (2) it enables cross-domain knowledge
transfer through a specialised memory structure; and (3) it adaptively reconfig-
ures agent allocations based on context-specific performance patterns. Impor-
tantly, each agent is built on a specialised neural or machine learning forecaster
(N-BEATS, TCN, BlockRNN, and LightGBM) rather than an LLM. We bor-
row the agentic principles of role specialisation, communication, memory, and
coordination from LLM-based multi-agent systems, while keeping the agents
lightweight, fast, and interpretable. Replacing or augmenting these agents with
LLM-based reasoning is a natural extension that we leave to future work. The
main contributions of this paper are:
1. A multi-agent architecture with specialised components for distinct aspects
of time series forecasting, with explicit communication protocols between
agents.

MACTS-EM Framework
MACTS-EM re-imagines time series forecasting as a collaborative process among
specialised agents coordinated by a meta-cognitive layer and an emergent mem-
ory mechanism (Fig. 1). Given a time series X = {x1, x2, . . . , xT } where xt ∈Rd,
our goal is to predict future values XT +1:T +H, considering optional covari-
ates Zp, Zf, and multimodal data M. Formally, MACTS-EM is defined as
M = (A, L, E, I, R), where A represents specialised agents, L the meta-cognitive
layer, E the emergent memory, I multimodal integration, and R adversarial ro-
bustness components.

2.4
Multimodal Integration and Robustness
The multimodal integration component processes diverse data types through
modality-specific encoders (BERT-based for text, CNN-based for visuals,
embedding-based for categorical data), combining them using cross-modal
attention and fusion to generate contextual representations for forecasting. The
adversarial robustness component enhances resilience by generating challenging
scenarios (level shifts, trend changes, volatility increases) and evaluating agent
performance under these conditions. A two-level weighting scheme integrates
meta-cognitive weights with robustness factors, with parameter γ controlling
adjustment strength. This enables MACTS-EM to maintain performance during
distribution shifts while defaulting to base weights during stable periods. After
evaluating multiple architectural alternatives (CNN, Transformer, and LSTM
variants) for each agent type, our final configuration was selected based on a
balance of accuracy, efficiency, and interpretability.
2.5
Training and Inference
MACTS-EM employs a two-stage training procedure: (1) independent agent pre-
training on domain-specific tasks, and (2) collaborative training with knowledge
sharing and meta-cognitive coordination. The ensemble loss function Lensemble =
Lforecast + λ1Ldiversity + λ2Lrobustness + λ3Ltransfer balances forecasting accuracy
with diversity, robustness, and transfer capabilities. During inference, MACTS-
EM follows a structured pipeline from input processing through agent selection,
forecasting, and aggregation to produce final predictions with uncertainty esti-
mates and explanations.

3.2
Baselines and Implementation
We compared MACTS-EM against state-of-the-art forecasting models: statistical
approaches (ARIMA, Prophet), deep learning models (N-BEATS [14], DeepAR,
Informer [27], TFT, TiDE [6], TimeMixer [21]), LLM-based and foundation mod-
els (Time-LLM [11], Chronos [2]), and a stacking ensemble of the strongest base-
lines.
Implementation used Python 3.11 with Darts 0.35.0, nixtla 0.6.6, PyTorch
2.0.1, and standard data science libraries. Experiments ran on 4 NVIDIA
A100 GPUs with hyperparameter optimisation via Bayesian methods (100 tri-
als/model), walk-forward time series cross-validation, and domain-specific train,
validation, and test splits to respect temporal ordering. For computational effi-
ciency, we implemented selective agent activation, incremental memory updates,
and parallel execution, reducing inference time by 42% compared to a naive
implementation.

Domain-Specific Performance MACTS-EM demonstrates distinctive advan-
tages across the evaluated domains:
Financial Markets: MACTS-EM achieves 7.3% improvement during stable
periods, with its greatest advantage during volatile periods (38.8% improvement
vs. TimeMixer during the COVID-19 crash). The anomaly detection agent pro-
vides early warning signals 3–4 days before significant movements, with news
sentiment integration contributing 25% of this improvement.
Climate Forecasting: Long-term projections show 12.0% lower error rates,
with 27.3% improvement for Arctic sea ice predictions. Satellite imagery integra-
tion reduces error by 18.2% during summer melting periods, while the anomaly
detection weight increases from 15% to 37% during extreme events, enabling
rapid adaptation.
Pandemic Propagation: MACTS-EM provides earlier variant detection
(2.3 days earlier) and more accurate peak timing (±1.8 days vs. ±4.6 days for
baselines). Agent weights shift dynamically, with mobility data integration re-
ducing MAPE by 24.7% during intervention periods, while cross-region knowl-
edge transfer provides a 3.5-day early detection advantage.

Multimodal Integration and Ablation Studies
Impact Assessment: MACTS-EM achieves 1.6–1.7× greater MAPE reduction
from multimodal integration compared to baselines (Table 5). The strongest
improvements are observed in energy forecasting with weather data (10.3%),
followed by pandemic forecasting with mobility data (9.2%), climate forecasting
with satellite imagery (7.8%), and financial forecasting with news sentiment
(6.9%).
Component Analysis: Ablation results (Table 5) reveal the meta-cognitive
layer as most critical (13.4% MAPE increase when removed), followed by
emergent memory (11.1%) and multimodal integration (10.1%). Among agents,
Anomaly Detection has the highest impact (9.3%), followed by Causal Infer-
ence (8.7%). These results quantify the contribution of each component to
overall performance, indicating that the framework’s advantages stem from the
interaction of its components rather than any single innovation.

Computational Considerations: MACTS-EM requires 36% more training
time than TimeMixer, primarily due to the collaborative training stage. However,
optimisations including selective agent activation (22% speedup), incremental
memory updates (15% speedup), and parallel execution (18% speedup) cre-
ate a favourable performance–efficiency trade-off. For medium-term forecasts,
optimised MACTS-EM requires 285 ms on average compared to 201 ms for
TimeMixer.
The computational complexity follows O(T · (nA · CA + CM + CI)), where T
is input sequence length, nA is agent count, CA is average agent complexity, CM
is meta-cognitive layer complexity, and CI is integration overhead. For real-time
applications, our streamlined MACTS-EM-RT variant achieves 3.7× faster infer-
ence with only 2.3% accuracy reduction. For edge deployment, 8-bit quantised
versions reduce memory requirements by 73% while maintaining performance
(< 1% accuracy loss).

5
Implications and Limitations
MACTS-EM represents a shift from monolithic models to collaborative agent-
based systems, with theoretical and practical implications. Our results demon-
strate that specialised expertise with intelligent coordination outperformed
general-purpose models, with the meta-cognitive structure mirroring human
cognitive architectures and enabling contextual adaptation. The emergent mem-
ory’s ability to transfer knowledge across domains suggests shared pattern
structures that transcend domain boundaries.
From a practical perspective, the framework offers valuable capabilities for:
early warning systems for market crashes and disease outbreaks (2–4 day ad-
vantages); resource allocation during rapid shifts in healthcare and energy man-
agement; cross-domain knowledge transfer enabling improved forecasts in data-
scarce contexts; and interpretable predictions with transparent agent contribu-
tions.
Despite strong performance, MACTS-EM has four key limitations: (1) in-
creased computational requirements (36% training and approximately 42%
inference overhead relative to TimeMixer), though our MACTS-EM-RT variant
reduces this through model distillation; (2) domain-specific underperformance
in short-term climate forecasting (−1.8%), medium-term energy forecasting
(−0.9%), Pandemic→Energy transfer (−1.1%), and energy distribution-shift
resilience (−2.9%); (3) an expanded hyperparameter space requiring careful
tuning; and (4) diminished performance for very long-term forecasts beyond 90
days. We observed that domains with strong cyclical patterns generally benefited
more than those with higher stochasticity, suggesting room for improvement in
handling highly irregular time series.
6
Conclusion and Future Directions
This paper introduced MACTS-EM, a collaborative forecasting framework
that re-imagines time series prediction through specialised agents coordinated
by a meta-cognitive layer. Our evaluation demonstrates improvements over
state-of-the-art models in most scenarios. The framework’s strongest advantages
emerge during challenging situations—market crashes (38.8% improvement),
pandemic waves (earlier variant detection), and extreme weather events (23.7–
28.4% improvement)—precisely when accurate forecasting is most critical. By
decomposing forecasting into specialised roles with coordinated information

Future research directions include architectural advances (hierarchical struc-

for optimising agent communication protocols and meta-learning for faster adap-
